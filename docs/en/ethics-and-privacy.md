# Ethics and privacy: running a fair 360

A 360 evaluation is only as good as the trust people have in it. These rules are built into the tool; the rest is up to you.

## Why you are doing it (say it before you start)
- To see how everyone has worked this period, from every side, not just one manager's.
- To find the good work that goes unseen.
- To give each person a clear plan for development and support.
- **Not** to punish, and **not** as the only basis for pay or contracts. If results will inform decisions, say so honestly,
  and always read them with other evidence and a conversation.

## What people are promised (and what the tool guarantees)
| Promise | How it is kept |
|---|---|
| The person you rate will not know it was you. | Employee copies contain no names. Groups smaller than the minimum (default 3) are merged into "others", and a person with fewer raters gets no score summary at all. |
| Only the admin sees individual answers. | Results live in the admin's sheet and a private folder; nothing is shared automatically. |
| Your written notes may reach the person, without your name. | Tell people this. Ask them to write kindly, without details that reveal who wrote it. |
| "I don't know" never counts against anyone. | It is not averaged in. |
| You only rate people you really work with. | Every pairing has a written reason, and nobody is paired at random. |
| High and low scores need a reason. | 1, 2 and 5 require a real example: what happened, when, the result. |
| People can take their time. | Answers save automatically and can be edited until the deadline. |

## The admin's own responsibilities
- **Keep the sheet private.** Do not share it, and do not add editors. Anyone with edit access can see every answer.
- **Know who else could look.** Your organisation's Google Workspace super administrators can technically open any
  file in your domain, this one included. If the admin of this evaluation is not also the Workspace administrator,
  tell staff honestly who could see what.
- **Do not guess raters aloud**, even to be helpful.
- **Read flags as questions, not verdicts.** The "manager may be blaming the team" signal is a reason to read the
  examples and talk, not a conclusion.
- **Treat the manager-accountability answers fairly.** A low score after the manager never discussed the issue says as
  much about the manager as about the member.
- **Share each report in a conversation**:
  - start with strengths;
  - then one or two areas to grow;
  - agree a plan together;
  - give the person space to respond in writing (the report has a place for it).
- **Delete what you no longer need.** After the conversations, keep the reports only as long as your policy requires.

## Personal data
- The answers, ratings and comments stay in your Google account. The tool has no server and sends them nowhere else.
- Your organisation is responsible for it, as with any HR record. Check your local data-protection rules, for example on
  telling staff what is collected and how long it is kept.
- If you use Claude Code to set up, what you type or paste into Claude (names, work emails, who manages whom) is sent to
  Anthropic so Claude can answer you. Claude never needs ratings or comments; do not paste them. The prepared files are
  in the `org` folder on your computer, which is never uploaded to GitHub. Delete it when you no longer need it.

## A suggested announcement (adapt it)
> Next week each of us will receive a personal invitation to our 360 evaluation. We will each rate ourselves, and be rated
> by our manager and the colleagues we work with, so we see our work from every side. It is confidential: only
> [admin] sees individual answers, and nobody will know who wrote what. Please be honest, fair and kind, with real
> examples. It takes about 10 minutes for yourself and 3 minutes per colleague, and your answers save as you go.
> Thank you.
