# Running the evaluation (admin guide)

## A typical timeline
| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2 weeks before | Set up, check, pairings, publish, preview. Tell the team in a meeting why you are doing it (see [Ethics and privacy](ethics-and-privacy.md)). |
| Day 1 | Send the invitations. Post a short message in your team chat. Hand out paper forms. |
| Days 1–10 | Watch **Progress**. One friendly reminder a few days before the deadline. |
| Deadline + 1 day | Enter paper forms. **Update results.** Read **Flags**, write decisions. |
| Week after | **Make the reports.** Read each admin copy. Plan conversations. |
| Following weeks | One conversation per person, using their employee copy. Agree a development plan. |

## During the evaluation
- **Update results** → tab **Progress**: who has finished, their percentage, and exactly whom each person still has to rate.
- **Send reminders to people who have not finished**: one email each, with what is left. It asks you to confirm first.
- **People can edit** their answers until the deadline. Their latest answer counts.
- **Answers save automatically**, so someone who stops halfway loses nothing.

## Paper forms
1. **Make paper forms for people without email** creates one Google Doc per person, in a new folder. Print them.
2. When they come back in sealed envelopes: **Enter answers from paper forms** opens a window with one link per person.
   Click the name. Only the admin can open these links.
3. Type the answers exactly as written and send. The answer is marked as entered by you.

## Reading the results (admin only)
| Tab | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Flags** | Ratings worth a second look, with the rater's name. See below. |
| **Review decisions** | Your word on a doubtful rating: rater email, rated email, **keep** or **exclude**. It beats the automatic rules. «Update results» never clears it; only «Start a new round» does. |
| **Results by person** | Averages from others and from self, the gap, strengths, areas to develop, and whether results can be shared (at least the minimum group). |
| **Heat map** | Everyone × every item, coloured. |
| **Departments** | How each department is seen by the others, plus the department matrix. |
| **Recognition** | Who colleagues named for help that is not always seen; "quiet stars". |
| **Managers and teams** | How each manager rates the team compared with everyone else, and how the team rates the leadership. |
| **Manager accountability** | Per team member: were expectations set, were weaknesses discussed, what the manager did, what the member says. |
| **Summary** | One line per person with a first indication. **Never a decision on its own.** |
| **Dashboard** | Charts for the whole organisation. |

### What the flags mean
| Flag | What happened | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Same score on every item | A rater gave 1 (or 5) to everything. Excluded automatically. | Read their examples. Keep it only if it is truly justified. |
| Rating nobody asked for | Someone rated a person not on their list. Excluded automatically. | Usually leave it out. |
| Rating far from the other raters | One rater is much lower or higher than everyone else. | Read the examples; it may be a real, different view. |
| Possible mutual flattery | Two colleagues gave each other very high scores. | Read the examples. |
| Manager may be blaming the team | The manager rates the team much lower than others do, while the team rates the leadership low. | A signal to look closer, not a verdict. |
| Criticism without follow-up | Low scores, but the weakness was never discussed with the person. | Weighs on the manager more than on the member. |
| Expectations were never set | Nothing was agreed at the start. | Do not judge "achieving what was expected" for this person. |
| Blind spot | Someone rates themselves much higher than others do. | A gentle conversation topic. |
| Seen as slow to respond | Several raters gave low responsiveness scores. | Read the examples. |

## Reports
**Make the reports** creates a private folder next to your sheet with three kinds of report:
- **Admin copy per person:** everything, including examples by group and the manager-accountability section.
- **Employee copy per person:**
  - what you can hand over, in a conversation;
  - no rater names, no warnings, constructive wording;
  - a chart of how they see themselves against how others see them;
  - strengths, areas to develop, what colleagues said;
  - a space for the agreed plan and signatures.
  If fewer than the minimum group rated someone, their copy says so, and you review their work in a conversation instead.
- **Organisation report:** participation, the main weaknesses across the organisation, departments, managers, quiet
  stars, and the heat map.

Nothing is shared with anyone. You decide, person by person.

**Large teams:** Google stops any single run after 6 minutes, and each person takes a few seconds. If the message says reports were made for only some people so far, choose **Make the reports** again: it continues in the same folder where it stopped.

## After the results: conversations, actions and follow-up
1. **Prepare.** Each admin copy ends with a short conversation guide. Read it before each conversation.
2. **Hold one conversation per person**, with their employee copy. It ends with a table for two or three agreed
   actions: what, the support they need, and by when.
3. **Write the agreed actions** in the **Agreed actions** tab. It is made with the reports, with one starting row per
   person; add a row for each extra action. Write "yes" under **Done** when an action is finished.
4. **Put the follow-up in your calendar.** When the reports are ready, the window offers **Add a reminder to my Google
   Calendar** for about 3 months later. One click, and it opens your calendar with the reminder filled in.
5. **Follow up.** On that day, choose **Follow up agreed actions**. After you confirm, each manager gets one email with
   the actions of their team that are not done yet. Actions for someone without a manager come to you. Actions stay in
   the tab across rounds until they are marked done; each round adds its own starting rows (the **Round** column).

## After changing the code or the questions
If you (or Claude) changed the code, publish a new version so people see it:
1. **Extensions → Apps Script → Deploy → Manage deployments.**
2. Press the pencil. Under **Version**, choose **New version**, then **Deploy**.
3. Check that the dialog shows a *new* version number. The dropdown can quietly stay on an old one. The page address
   does not change.

Changing the Questions or Settings tabs needs no new version.

## Starting a new round next year
**Either keep the same sheet:**
1. **360 evaluation → Start a new round**.
2. Update the round name and deadline in **Settings**.
3. Check the **Team** tab.
4. **3) Make the pairings**.
5. **5) Send me a preview**, then **6) Send the invitations**.

The personal page keeps its address.

**Or keep last year's results untouched in their own file:**
1. **File → Make a copy** of the sheet and open the copy.
2. In the copy: **360 evaluation → Start a new round**.
3. Follow the steps above, and also **4) Publish the personal page** again: the copy gets its own page with a new address.

"Start a new round" clears answers, unsent drafts, review decisions, pairings and results. It keeps the team, work
links, questions and settings, and asks you first.
